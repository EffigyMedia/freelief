### AUD-103 - Breathing and the Visualizer hold a wake lock with no end, even when paused, so a phone left on them never sleeps

- Severity: Medium
- Area: F. User experience
- Evidence: Quoted in round UNT-082. exercises/breathe.js: `wake: ctx.keepAwake()` is released only in `stop()` (`state.wake()`). `setPaused` clears the timers and the tone but not the lock. wakelock.js takes the lock again on every `visibilitychange`. Design section 8: wakelock.js holds "The screen wake lock while breathing or the Visualizer runs", and F5: "The screen stays awake while it runs." Neither exercise has an end ("No exercise has an end state"), and section 2 says use is "often at night". No clause says what happens when the person stops paying attention or falls asleep. (quoted in round UNT-082)
- Impact: A person who calms down and falls asleep on Breathe or the Visualizer, which is a likely outcome at night, leaves the screen on and sound playing for hours. The phone can be flat by morning, and it is also the phone they would use to call a crisis line. A paused breathing screen also keeps the phone awake for no reason.
- Direction: Design concern for the owner. Release the wake lock while breathing is paused. Decide a rule for long use, for example release the lock after a configurable time with no input while the exercise goes on. That is not a timer the person must beat, so REQ-011 still holds. Put the time in config.json and record the decision.
- Effort: S
- Found by: the omission lens of round [[UNT-082]].
- **RULED 2026-10-09T08:46:02-04:00** — 2026-10-09, owner ruling by the question tool: the wake lock is released after 30 minutes with no touch or key by default, and a setting chooses 10, 30 or 60 minutes. (Offered: release after 30 min idle; on pause only; after 10 min idle.) Pausing breathing releases it as well.
