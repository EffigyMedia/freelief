### AUD-180 - Unconfirmed: on iPhone, sound may not come back after the person calls a crisis line and returns, because only a 'suspended' audio context is resumed

- Severity: Low
- Area: none - omission (an actor path that nothing handles: a call and the return)
- Evidence: Quoted in round UNT-139. audio.js ensureContext(): "if (context.state === \"suspended\" && !held) context.resume();". A grep for "interrupted" finds nothing in any .js file, the design or the technical references. WebKit gives an AudioContext the state 'interrupted' during a phone call or another app's audio session. The help dialog's main action is "tel:" links ("Call {number}"), and the visibilitychange handler on return calls audio.applySound(true), which reaches ensureContext. No test runs this path, and no RLG-033 check names it. Confidence: low to medium. WebKit may resume by itself when the interruption ends, and this round did not check that on a device. (quoted in round UNT-139)
- Impact: A person who calls a line and comes back to breathing may get no breath sound and no background sound, and sound may stay silent until a reload. This is a small harm, but it falls at a fragile moment. The code then also schedules notes on a clock that is not running.
- Direction: Add an owner check to RLG-033: on an iPhone, with music and breathing sound on, tap Call, end the call, and come back; does sound return? If it does not, treat 'interrupted' like 'suspended' on the visible event and on the next gesture, and record the case in the design (section 8, audio.js).
- Effort: S
- Found by: the omission lens of round [[UNT-139]].

No automated test: Chrome has no 'interrupted' state. The device check is added to RLG-033; in-flight until a round and that check.
