### AUD-081 - A wake lock granted after the screen has already released it is kept, so the screen stays on after leaving breathing or the Visualizer

- Severity: Low
- Area: C. Edge cases (interruption mid-operation)
- Evidence: wakelock.js (quoted in round UNT-082): `sentinel = await navigator.wakeLock.request("screen");` sets the sentinel without checking `wanted` again after the await. The releaser does `if (!wanted && sentinel) { sentinel.release()...; sentinel = null; }`, so it releases nothing while the request is still pending. Two acquires in flight, from keepAwake() and the visibilitychange listener, can also overwrite `sentinel` and lose the first lock. Scratch probe: a WAKE_PROBE-style stub whose request resolves after 300 ms. Go to #breathe, then at once to #menu, and wait 1 s. Output: 'on menu after 1 s: held = 1 requests = 1'. The suite's own WAKE_PROBE resolves at once, so test_breathing_and_the_visualizer_keep_the_screen_awake_and_let_it_sleep_after cannot see this. (quoted in round UNT-082)
- Impact: This happens when a person leaves breathing or the Visualizer within the request's latency, or on a visibility change during a request. The phone then does not dim or lock on the menu or the other screens until the page is hidden. That drains the battery and goes against the design's rule that only breathing and the Visualizer keep the screen awake. The impact is small and it corrects itself when the page is hidden.
- Direction: After the await, release the new sentinel at once if `wanted` is 0 or if another sentinel is already held. Keep one in-flight request promise so two acquires do not both request. Add a test with a delayed request stub.
- Effort: S
