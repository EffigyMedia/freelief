### AUD-161 - Two agreed requirements, REQ-007 and REQ-034, still name the removed trace and colour sort, and the sweep rule does not list REQ- records

- Severity: Medium
- Area: A. Requirement compliance / design alignment
- Evidence: Quoted in round UNT-139. REQ-007 (agreed, should; in SRS.md clause 3.2): 'short cues for the activities (a bubble pop, a singing-glass tone while tracing, a soft tick and a rising phrase in Unblock, ...)'. It does not name Zen Garden's sand sound. REQ-034 (agreed, could): 'a short vibration on a single triggered event in an activity (a pop, a choice, a swap, a finished sort, a finished loop, a ripple, a fill)'. Against these, the Decision Log says 'The trace, its twelve shapes and the crystal-glass sound are removed; REQ-013 is withdrawn' (UNT-115). REQ-014 is withdrawn. config.json haptics.patterns has no 'loop' (AUD-145). The haptic calls are pop, choose, fill, ripple, done and swap. The AGENTS.md sweep rule names 'the design (...), the technical references, the `config.json` groups, the strings, the issue templates in `.github/`, `docs/README.md`, and the owed checks in RLG-033', but not the REQ- records. AGENTS.md Conventions: 'A changed requirement is edited in its own `REQ-` record with its history beneath it'. (quoted in round UNT-139)
- Impact: The SRS that the generator publishes specifies a singing-glass tone and loop and sort vibrations that no screen has. It omits the sound that Zen Garden plays. A reader who builds or verifies from the requirements (REQ-007's method is 'test') checks for removed behaviour. The next screen removal will miss the REQ- records again.
- Direction: Edit REQ-007 to replace the singing-glass cue with Zen Garden's sand sound, and edit REQ-034 to drop 'a finished sort' and 'a finished loop'. Add a history line to each record. Regenerate SRS.md and PRD.md. Add 'the agreed REQ- records' to the sweep list in AGENTS.md, log it in the Instruction Changelog, and extend test_repo's RETIRED_NAMES check to agreed REQ- records ('tracing', 'sort', 'loop').
