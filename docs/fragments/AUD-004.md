### AUD-004 - Freelief shares its web origin with three other sites, and any of them can rewrite its offline cache and settings

- Severity: Medium
- Area: E. Security
- Evidence: Quoted this round. `gh api 'users/EffigyMedia/repos?per_page=100' --jq '.[] | select(.has_pages) | .name'` returned `drinax-ref-console`, `effigy-arcade`, `freelief` and `tiny-arcade`. All four are served from https://effigymedia.github.io, which is one origin. Cache Storage and localStorage belong to the origin, not to the path. Probe (scratch script, harness server): a page at `/docs/` ran `caches.open(keys[0])` and then `c.put('/', new Response('...TAMPERED'))`. It saw `cache keys seen from /docs/: ['freelief-0.5.0']`. The app root then showed `after tamper, root shows: TAMPERED`, because `sw.js` answers cache first: `caches.match(event.request, { ignoreSearch: true }).then((cached) => cached || fetch(event.request))`. The design's Security and privacy section says "A Content Security Policy that allows only the app's own origin backs up the no-network rule". But `script-src 'self'` here also allows scripts from the three sibling sites. (quoted in round UNT-010)
- Impact: A defect or compromise in any sibling site (an XSS in a game, a bad dependency there) can replace Freelief's cached index.html or crisis-lines.json for every visitor who opens that sibling. The change persists offline until the next version bump, so a person in a panic attack could see wrong crisis numbers or a hostile page. Freelief's security boundary in practice includes three other apps, and the design does not say so.
- Direction: Serve Freelief from an origin of its own. A custom domain on its Pages site would do it, as would a dedicated account or organization whose `<name>.github.io` root is Freelief. Until then, record the shared origin in the design's Security and privacy section as a known trust dependency, with a Decision Log entry, and correct the claim that the CSP allows only the app's own origin.
