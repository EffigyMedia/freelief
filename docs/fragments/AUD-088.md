### AUD-088 - The design's first tie-breaker principle and its problem statement call a menu an obstacle to help, while the owner's primary flow opens on a menu

- Severity: Medium
- Area: A. Design alignment
- Evidence: Quoted in round UNT-082 at 82a1c6d. Design section 3: "**Principles (tie-breakers, in order):** 1. **Help first.** Nothing stands between the person and help: no question, menu, notice or wait." Section 1: "The apps that offer these techniques often ask for an account, show a menu, need a network..." The prd-problem region, regenerated into PRD.md: "The tools that exist put obstacles in the way at the worst moment: a sign-up, a menu, a loading screen..." Against these: REQ-018 "When Freelief opens, it shows the menu of exercises and activities at once", F1 "Launch to the menu", and the Decision Log (UNT-061): "since the app opens on the menu, a person in panic must read and choose before any help starts. The owner kept the menu as the default". AUD-064 fixed AGENTS.md, F1 and Stages 2, 6 and 8, but its list did not include section 3 or the problem text. (quoted in round UNT-082)
- Impact: Principle 1 is the first tie-breaker. A later session or audit lens that applies the principles in order rules against the owner's menu-first decision and may move the launch back to breathing. The public PRD names a menu as an obstacle, and the product opens on one.
- Direction: Restate Principle 1 to match the owner's decision. For example: nothing to answer before help, the menu first with Breathe one tap away, and an Open on setting for zero taps. Remove 'show a menu' and 'a menu' from section 1 and the prd-problem region, or say that the obstacle is a menu that asks for something. Delete the old wording in the same edit, add a Decision Log note, and regenerate the PRD.
- Effort: S
