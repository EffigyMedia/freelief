### RLG-024 — Haptic feedback on triggered events, a Settings toggle on by default
- **RULED 2026-10-08T00:25:19-04:00** — Built in UNT-046 as v0.5.32. haptics.js pulse(name) sends one pattern from config.json haptics.patterns when the haptics setting is on and navigator.vibrate exists, in try/catch. The shell passes it to every screen as ctx.haptic. Pulses: bubble pop, sort choose (tap and drag start), swap and done, trace loop, ripple from a touch or a key (never the drag trail), mandala fill. Settings has a Vibration switch, on by default (haptics.enabledByDefault), with a hint that says it does not work on iPhone or in Safari (owner). test_haptics.py: one pattern per event, no pulse over a full breathing cycle or a drag trail, the switch by keyboard turns it off and is saved, and no error without the API. The round trip includes haptics. The trace loop pulse is not covered by a test, because completing a loop in a test is slow; it shares the loop cue's line. 110 tests. How the buzz feels is for the owner to check on an Android phone.
- **RULED 2026-10-08T00:27:08-04:00** — Amended in UNT-047 as v0.5.33, after the owner asked whether 'iPhone or Safari' was right. Source checked in this session: caniuse.com, Vibration API. Safari on macOS and on iOS: not supported, so no browser on iPhone supports it (all use WebKit). Firefox desktop: supported to 128, not from 129; Firefox for Android: not supported. Chrome desktop, Chrome for Android and Samsung Internet: supported. The hint now reads: 'It works in Chrome and Samsung Internet on Android. It does not work on iPhone, in Safari or in Firefox.'
- **RULED 2026-10-08T00:31:03-04:00** — Amended in UNT-048 as v0.5.34 (owner: smaller or less verbose, or both). The hint reads 'A short buzz on taps. Android only (Chrome, Samsung Internet), not iPhone.' at 0.9rem (small-hint), still in the muted color that meets 7:1. 'Android only' covers Safari on a Mac, which has no vibration motor, and Firefox for Android is not in the list of browsers named. The test checks the words and that the hint is smaller than body text; axe passes. 110 tests.
