### AUD-162 - The installed app is locked to portrait, but the decision never weighs WCAG 2.2 SC 1.3.4 Orientation, which REQ-016 requires in full

- Severity: Medium
- Area: none - omission (a decision with no reconciliation against a Must requirement)
- Evidence: Quoted in round UNT-139. manifest.webmanifest: "orientation": "portrait". test_offline.py: "assert manifest[\"orientation\"] == \"portrait\", \"owner, 2026-10-07: portrait only once installed\"". Decision Log: "The installed app is portrait only. — Owner request. ... Rejected: a 'turn your phone' screen in landscape". REQ-016 (must): "Freelief meets WCAG 2.2 level AA in full". SC 1.3.4 Orientation is level AA: content must not restrict its view to one orientation unless that orientation is essential. A grep for "1.3.4" and "Orientation" over the design, the records and the tests finds only the portrait decision. No record names the conflict or calls portrait essential. (quoted in round UNT-139)
- Impact: A person whose phone is mounted in landscape, for example on a wheelchair, cannot use the installed app in that orientation on Android. That is the user 1.3.4 protects. The axe check cannot see the manifest, so the gap is invisible to every automated check, and the app quietly misses the accessibility target it was built for.
- Direction: Put the conflict to the owner as one question. Either remove the orientation key, or keep it and record it as a stated exception to REQ-016 with the owner's reason, in REQ-016's history and in the Decision Log entry. Change the manifest test to match the ruling.
- Effort: S
- Found by: the omission lens of round [[UNT-139]].
